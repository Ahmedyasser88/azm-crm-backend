## Dependency notes

All four stories implement KAN-1 ("Customer Management Module"), split by aggregate/sub-resource so each is independently reviewable and testable:

- **Story 01** creates the `Customer` entity, `IApplicationDbContext.Customers`, and `CustomersController` — every other story depends on it.
- **Stories 02-04** each add one child aggregate (`CustomerInteraction`, `CustomerNote`, `CustomerAttachment`) with its own `HasOne(...).WithMany()` FK relationship back to `Customer`. They only depend on Story 01, not on each other, but are numbered sequentially (02 → 03 → 04) because each edits the same shared files incrementally:
  - `src/AzmCrm.Application/Shared/Interfaces/IApplicationDbContext.cs` (one new `DbSet<T>` member per story)
  - `src/AzmCrm.Infrastructure/Data/ApplicationDbContext.cs` (one new `DbSet<T>` property per story)
  - `src/AzmCrm.API/Controllers/CustomersController.cs` (new actions appended per story)
  - `tests/AzmCrm.Application.Tests/TestApplicationDbContext.cs` (one new `DbSet<T>` property per story, introduced in Story 01's Test Plan)

  Implementing them out of numeric order (e.g. 04 before 02) is possible but will produce a slightly different, still-correct, diff to the shared files above — the plans do not assume a specific merge order beyond "after Story 01".
- Story 02 additionally changes `services.AddControllers()` in `src/AzmCrm.API/Extensions/ApplicationExtensions.cs` to register a `JsonStringEnumConverter` (needed for `InteractionType`). This is a global JSON serialization change; Stories 03 and 04 don't depend on it but will inherit it once Story 02 lands.
- Story 04 introduces `IFileStorageService` (Application) / `LocalFileStorageService` (Infrastructure) and a new `App_Data/attachments`-rooted local disk store, plus a new `AzmCrm.Infrastructure.Tests` test project (none currently exists). No other story depends on this.
- **Out of scope across all four KAN-1 stories** (see each story's Story Goal for specifics): hard/permanent delete, customer merge/de-duplication, export, editing/deleting a note or interaction, attachment deletion/versioning, and any integration with KAN-2 (Ticket Management) or KAN-3 (Communication Channels) — those are separate, not-yet-planned Jira stories that may become additional sources of interaction records once built.

Stories 05-07 implement KAN-2 ("Ticket Management System"), split the same way as KAN-1 — a core-CRUD story others depend on, plus independent extension stories that each edit the core story's shared files incrementally:

- **Story 05** creates the `Ticket` and `TicketHistory` entities, `IApplicationDbContext.Tickets`/`TicketHistories`, and `TicketsController` (`api/tickets`) — every other KAN-2 story depends on it. It also covers three of KAN-2's five acceptance criteria in one pass (create/track, categories/priorities, and the history-viewing endpoint), since those map directly onto fields on the `Ticket` entity itself and a single child `TicketHistory` table, mirroring how KAN-1 Story 01 folded "contact details" into its core CRUD story rather than splitting it out.
- **Stories 06-07** each depend only on Story 05 (not on each other) but are numbered sequentially because both edit the same shared files incrementally: `Ticket.cs` (new properties), `TicketDto.cs`/`TicketListItemDto.cs` (new trailing DTO parameters), `GetTicketsListQuery.cs`/`GetTicketsListQueryHandler.cs` (new optional filter), `TicketConfiguration.cs` (new column/index config), and `TicketsController.cs` (new actions). Implementing them out of order produces a different, still-correct diff to those files.
  - Story 06 adds `Ticket.AssignedToUserId` and validates it via the existing `IIdentityQueryService` abstraction (`src/AzmCrm.Application/Shared/Interfaces/IIdentityQueryService.cs`, already registered in DI but unused elsewhere in the codebase before this story) rather than exposing `ApplicationUser` through `IApplicationDbContext` — keeping Identity access behind that interface at the Application layer, while `TicketConfiguration` still declares a real DB-level FK to `ApplicationUser` since both live in the same `ApplicationDbContext`.
  - Story 07 adds `Ticket.IsEscalated`/`EscalatedOn` and two new commands (`ChangeTicketStatusCommand`, `EscalateTicketCommand`); it reuses `Ticket.Status` and the `StatusChanged`/`Escalated` members of `TicketHistoryEventType`, both already defined by Story 05.
  - Every mutating command across Stories 05-07 (create, update, assign, unassign, status change, escalate) writes to the shared `TicketHistory` table, so Story 05's `GET /api/tickets/{id}/history` endpoint needs no further changes to fully satisfy "View complete ticket history" once Stories 06-07 land.
- **Out of scope across all three KAN-2 stories**: ticket deletion, a formal status state machine/transition rules, de-escalation, enforcing that an assigned `ApplicationUser` is an active agent, and assignment/escalation notifications.

Stories 08-12 implement KAN-3 ("Communication Channels Integration"), split into one foundational story plus four independent channel stories — each channel story depends only on Story 08 and can be implemented/merged in any order relative to the other three:

- **Story 08** creates the `Conversation`/`Message` aggregate (`src/AzmCrm.Domain/Features/Communications/`), `IApplicationDbContext.Conversations`/`Messages`, `ConversationsController`, and the `IChannelMessageSender` extension point every later channel story plugs into — every other KAN-3 story depends on it. It also fully satisfies one of KAN-3's five acceptance criteria on its own ("Accept submissions from web forms," via a public `POST /api/conversations/web-form` endpoint), the same way KAN-2 Story 05 folded three of its five acceptance criteria into one core-CRUD pass.
- **Stories 09-11** (Email, WhatsApp, SMS) each add one outbound `IChannelMessageSender` implementation plus one inbound webhook action on `ConversationsController`, following an identical internal shape (provider interface + Infrastructure implementation + settings class + "find-or-create customer, find-or-create open conversation for that channel" inbound handler + `ExternalMessageId`-based idempotency). None of them edit any file another one of these three creates — they only make small, additive, non-overlapping edits to three shared files: `src/AzmCrm.Infrastructure/DependencyInjection.cs` (one new registration block each), `src/AzmCrm.API/Controllers/ConversationsController.cs` (one or two new actions each), and `src/AzmCrm.API/appsettings.json` (one new top-level settings section each).
- **Story 12** (Live Chat) does not implement `IChannelMessageSender` at all — it adds a SignalR hub (`src/AzmCrm.API/Hubs/ChatHub.cs`) for push-based, bidirectional real-time delivery instead, plus a public `POST /api/conversations/live-chat/start` action. It edits `Program.cs` (maps the hub) and `ApplicationExtensions.cs`/`DependencyInjection.cs` (registers SignalR and a JWT-over-query-string auth hook) in addition to the same three shared files Stories 09-11 touch.
- **Out of scope across all four KAN-3 channel stories**: real, tested third-party provider credentials (Meta WhatsApp Cloud API, an SMTP host, an SMS gateway) — each story wires the abstraction and, where a specific provider is named, that provider's documented contract shape, but none of them have been exercised against a live account in this session; linking a `Conversation` to a `Ticket` (KAN-2); closing/reopening a conversation via a dedicated endpoint; editing or deleting a message; and (Story 12 specifically) a customer identity/session system stronger than "knows the conversation's Guid id," multi-instance SignalR scaling, and typing indicators/presence.

Stories 13-16 implement KAN-4 ("Agent Dashboard"), split into four independent stories — one per remaining acceptance criterion pairing, none of which edit a file another one of these four creates:

- **Story 13** covers two of KAN-4's five acceptance criteria in one pass ("View all assigned tickets in one place" and "Access customer information from the dashboard"), the same way KAN-2 Story 05 and KAN-3 Story 08 each folded multiple acceptance criteria into their core story. It's a pure read composition — a new `DashboardController` plus two new queries — over the existing `Ticket`/`Customer` tables; no new entity or migration.
- **Story 14** ("Manage tasks and reminders") adds a new `AgentTask` entity, `IApplicationDbContext.AgentTasks`, and `AgentTasksController` (`api/agent-tasks`). Tasks are strictly per-agent (every command/query filters by `AssignedToUserId == currentUserService.UserId`, and a task belonging to another agent 404s rather than 403s to avoid confirming its existence) and may optionally link to a `Customer` and/or `Ticket` by id.
- **Story 15** ("Use quick reply templates") adds a new `QuickReplyTemplate` entity, `IApplicationDbContext.QuickReplyTemplates`, and `QuickReplyTemplatesController` (`api/quick-reply-templates`). Templates are team-shared, not per-agent — any authenticated agent can create, edit, delete, or list any template — the opposite ownership model from Story 14's `AgentTask`, which is intentional and documented in both stories' Edge Cases.
- **Story 16** ("Collaborate with team members") adds a new `TicketComment` entity (a `CustomerNote`-shaped child of `Ticket`, alongside `TicketHistory`) and two new actions on the existing `TicketsController`: `POST/GET /api/tickets/{id}/comments`. Comments are append-only, ordered oldest-first (a collaboration thread reads like a chat log, mirroring KAN-3 Story 08's identical reasoning for conversation messages), and each comment's author name is resolved via the existing `IIdentityQueryService` batch-lookup pattern from KAN-2 Story 06.
- Story 13 depends on Story 06 (`Ticket.AssignedToUserId`) and Story 01 (`Customer`); Story 16 depends on Story 05 (`Ticket`); Stories 14 and 15 depend on nothing beyond what already exists in `master` (Story 14 optionally validates `CustomerId`/`TicketId` against Stories 01/05 when those optional fields are provided, but has no hard dependency on either).
- **Out of scope across all four KAN-4 stories**: a shared/team view of another agent's dashboard or tasks (Story 13's "my tickets" and Story 14's tasks are both strictly self-scoped — a supervisor view already exists via `GET /api/tickets?assignedToUserId=...` from KAN-2 Story 06 and is not extended here), wiring a Story 15 template directly into KAN-3 Story 08's `SendMessageCommand` (the frontend copies a template's body into the existing send-message call itself), editing/deleting a Story 16 comment once posted, recurring reminders or due-date notifications for Story 14 tasks, and any role-based restriction on who can create/edit a Story 15 template (every authenticated agent has equal access, matching this codebase's current lack of a role model beyond `ApplicationUser`).

Stories 17-20 implement KAN-5 ("SLA & Automation Engine"), one story per acceptance criterion, each depending on the previous for a shared file or a field it reads — the first story in this codebase to add a recurring background process (no Hangfire/Quartz is used; a plain ASP.NET Core `BackgroundService` with a `PeriodicTimer` polls on a configurable interval):

- **Story 17** ("Set response and resolution time targets") creates the `SlaPolicy` entity (one active policy per `TicketPriority`, `SlaPoliciesController` at `api/sla-policies`) and, its core contribution, edits `CreateTicketCommandHandler` to stamp every new ticket with `SlaPolicyId`/`ResponseDueOn`/`ResolutionDueOn` from the policy matching its priority, plus edits `ChangeTicketStatusCommandHandler` (Story 07) to stamp `Ticket.RespondedOn` the first time a ticket's status leaves `New` — this codebase's only "first response" signal, since there is no separate reply-tracking mechanism.
- **Story 18** ("Auto-assign tickets based on rules") creates the `AssignmentRule` entity (`Category`/`Priority`-scoped, one fixed target agent, ordered by `EvaluationOrder`; `AssignmentRulesController` at `api/assignment-rules`) under a new `src/AzmCrm.Domain/Features/Automation/` folder, and edits `CreateTicketCommandHandler` a second time (after Story 17's SLA-stamping block) to auto-assign a new ticket to the best-matching active rule's agent, logging a `TicketHistoryEventType.Assigned` row exactly as a manual `AssignTicketCommand` (Story 06) would.
- **Story 19** ("Configure escalation rules for overdue tickets") creates the `EscalationRule` entity (`Priority`-scoped grace period in minutes) and the actual recurring mechanism: `ScanSlaBreachesCommand`/Handler (Application layer, fully unit-testable via `TestApplicationDbContext` independent of any timer) finds open, non-escalated, overdue tickets and escalates them with the same effect as Story 07's `EscalateTicketCommand`; `SlaMonitoringBackgroundService` (Infrastructure layer, `services.AddHostedService<...>()`) drives that command on a `SlaMonitoring:IntervalMinutes`-configured interval (default 5).
- **Story 20** ("Trigger alerts and notifications for SLA breaches") adds the `SlaBreachNotification` entity/`SlaBreachType` enum and a read-only `SlaBreachNotificationsController` at `api/sla-breach-notifications`, then edits `ScanSlaBreachesCommandHandler` a second time (same scan tick, no new schedule) to also detect `ResponseDueOn` breaches (alert-only, fired once per ticket) and to persist+best-effort-email (reusing the existing `IEmailSender`/`SmtpEmailSender` from KAN-3 Story 09) a notification for every response breach and every resolution escalation it performs.
- Story 17 depends on Story 05 (`Ticket`) and Story 07 (`ChangeTicketStatusCommandHandler`, the file it edits for `RespondedOn`); Story 18 depends on Story 06 (`AssignedToUserId`/`IIdentityQueryService` precedent) and Story 17 (shares the `CreateTicketCommandHandler` edit point, sequentially); Story 19 depends on Story 07 (mirrors `EscalateTicketCommandHandler`'s effect) and Story 17 (`ResolutionDueOn`), and is written assuming Story 18 landed first only for the shared `Automation` folder/`IApplicationDbContext.cs` editing pattern, not for `AssignmentRule` itself; Story 20 depends on Story 19 (edits its `ScanSlaBreachesCommandHandler` and reuses its timer) and Story 17 (`ResponseDueOn`/`RespondedOn`).
- **Out of scope across all four KAN-5 stories**: per-category SLA targets (Story 17 keys policies by `TicketPriority` only); round-robin/load-balanced auto-assignment across a pool of agents (Story 18's rules each target exactly one fixed agent); re-evaluating assignment rules against tickets that already existed when a rule changes (Story 18 only applies at ticket-creation time); a manual "trigger scan now" endpoint or an operational kill-switch for the background service (Story 19); de-escalation via the automatic path; in-app/real-time push notifications and a "mark as read" action (Story 20 is email + a queryable REST list only); and re-sending a failed breach email.
